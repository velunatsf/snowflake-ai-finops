A

What Is a Token?

Every AI model processes text by breaking it into smaller pieces called tokens. These tokens are the fundamental billing unit for AI operations in Snowflake Cortex.

Token Basics

  • 1 token ≈ 0.75 English words
  • Common words = 1 token
  • Long/uncommon words = multiple tokens
  • Spaces and punctuation = tokens
  • Both input AND output count toward cost

Quick Estimates

  • 100 words ≈ 130 tokens
  • 1 page of text ≈ 500 tokens
  • 1 short email ≈ 100 to 200 tokens
  • 1 SQL query ≈ 50 to 150 tokens

Prompt + Response Anatomy

Every AI call has two token components that get billed:

PROMPT (Input Tokens)
System message : 50 tokens
Your instruction : 30 tokens
Data context : 200 tokens
RESPONSE (Output Tokens)
Model reply : 150 tokens
Total this call: 430 tokens

That is the whole billing model for a single one-shot call: count the tokens going in, count the tokens coming out, pay for both. Conversations add two more token types, which is the next section.

B

The Four Token Types on Your Bill

"Input and output" is the beginner's model of AI billing, and it is the right place to start. But once you hold a real conversation with an AI assistant, Snowflake reports four token types, not two. Learn the names now; you will see them in every usage view for the rest of the day.

Here is the intuition. Imagine you are working with a tutor and you hand them a thick reference binder at the start of the session. Reading that binder the first time takes real effort. On every later question, the tutor just glances back at the binder they already read. AI models work the same way, and Snowflake bills the two situations differently.

Token type What it means Cost behaviour
input New text you send that the model has not seen before in this session Standard input rate
output Text the model generates back to you Usually the most expensive per token
cache_write_input Context written into the prompt cache the first time it is processed, so later turns can reuse it. This is the tutor reading the binder. Paid once to seed the cache
cache_read_input Cached context reused on later turns of the same session. This is the tutor glancing back at the binder. Billed at a reduced rate
Why this matters more than it looks

In a long conversation, most of each prompt is unchanged history. If none of it is cached, you pay full price to re-read the same context on every single turn. Getting cache reuse right is the single largest lever in this training, which is why it gets its own dedicated session in Module 07: KV Cache Optimization. For now, just remember the four names.

Key insight for the whole day

Output tokens typically cost more than input tokens. A verbose answer can cost several times what a concise one costs for the same question. Asking the model to "answer in one sentence" is a genuine cost optimization, not just a style preference.

C

Two Cost Layers in Snowflake AI

When you run AI workloads in Snowflake, you're dealing with two distinct cost layers. Understanding this distinction is crucial for FinOps.

Layer 1: Warehouse Compute Credits

  • Traditional Snowflake billing
  • Based on warehouse size × time running
  • Stops when warehouse suspends
  • You control this via auto-suspend settings
Example: SMALL warehouse = 2 credits/hour

Layer 2: AI Token Credits (Cortex)

  • New billing layer for AI functions
  • Based on tokens consumed per call
  • Does NOT stop when warehouse suspends
  • Some functions run serverless
Example: AI Credits are billed flat (~$2.00 global / $2.20 regional per AI Credit) - separate from Platform Credits for warehouses
Critical Cost Warning

Token costs do not stop when you suspend your warehouse. A misconfigured loop calling AI_COMPLETE() across 1 million rows can exceed a week of warehouse compute in minutes. This is the #1 surprise cost for teams adopting Cortex AI.

Serverless vs. Warehouse-Based

Execution Mode Functions Cost Behavior
Serverless AI_COMPLETE, AI_SENTIMENT, AI_SUMMARIZE_AGG, AI_TRANSLATE, AI_CLASSIFY Token credits only - no warehouse needed
Warehouse + Serverless Cortex Search, Cortex Agents / CoWork Both warehouse compute AND token credits
Warehouse-based FORECAST, ANOMALY_DETECTION Primarily warehouse compute
D

From Tokens to Dollars

Snowflake never bills you in tokens directly. Tokens convert to credits, and credits convert to dollars. There are only three numbers in the chain.

STEP 1 · TOKENS
You send 500 prompt tokens and get 200 response tokens = 700 tokens
STEP 2 · CREDITS
Each model has a published tokens-per-credit rate. At roughly 826,000 tokens
per credit, 700 tokens costs about 0.00085 AI Credits.
STEP 3 · DOLLARS
One AI Credit is about $2.00, so this call costs roughly $0.0017.
Run it 1,000,000 times and it is about $1,700.

The per-call number is always tiny. The scale factor is what gets teams into trouble, which is exactly why we estimate before we run.

AI Credits and Platform Credits are priced differently

AI Credits cover AISQL, Agents, CoWork, CoCo, and Search. They use a flat price that does not depend on your Snowflake edition: about $2.00 with global routing or $2.20 with regional routing. See Snowflake AI pricing.

Platform Credits cover warehouses, storage, and non-AI services, and they do vary by edition and region, often in the $2.50 to $4.00 range. When you dollarize AI spend, use the AI Credit price. Always confirm against your own contract and the Credit Consumption Table.

Rates vary enormously by model and function

The tokens-per-credit rate above is one example. Across the model catalog the spread between the cheapest and most expensive model is 10x to 50x for the same prompt, and several Snowflake functions are not billed per token at all. Those rate tables are the reference material for Module 02: The Snowflake AI Toolbox, so we keep them in one place rather than repeating them here.

Knowledge Check

Q1: A 500-token prompt generates a 300-token response. How many total tokens are billed?

800 tokens total. Both input (prompt) and output (response) tokens are billed. This is why limiting response length is a cost optimization technique.

Q2: Which cost layer stops when you suspend your warehouse?

Layer 1: Warehouse Compute Credits. Layer 2 (AI Token Credits) continues to accumulate on every Cortex function call, regardless of warehouse state. Some Cortex functions run serverless and don't use a warehouse at all.

Q3: What does "serverless Cortex" mean for cost tracking?

You pay only for tokens, not warehouse time. Functions like AI_SENTIMENT(), AI_COMPLETE(), and AI_TRANSLATE() run on Snowflake's managed infrastructure. This is efficient for sporadic use, but can accumulate rapidly at scale since there's no auto-suspend safety net.

Q4: Name the four token types Snowflake reports, and say which one is billed at a reduced rate.

input, output, cache_write_input, and cache_read_input. cache_read_input is the discounted one, because the model is reusing context it already processed earlier in the session. Module 07 turns that into a KPI you can track weekly.

Q5: Your team already has a Resource Monitor on the warehouse. Is your AI spend capped?

No. Resource Monitors cap warehouse credits only. AI Credits need their own controls: tag-based AI feature budgets, CoCo per-user credit limits, and model access control. You build all three in Module 08.

Q6: You need to summarize 2 million support tickets. What do you do before writing the query?

Estimate first. Use the estimator in Section E with a realistic prompt and response length, then compare a Budget model against a Premium one. A model that costs a fraction of a cent per call still costs thousands of dollars at two million calls.
